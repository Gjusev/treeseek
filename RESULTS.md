# RESULTS.md

Measured on 2026-11-02, one run of `python run.py --build` followed by `python run.py --eval --retriever both`, on CPU (Windows 11, Python 3.10). Corpus: 3000 passages from the 20 Newsgroups train split, 150 per topic. Query set: 80 held-out subject-line queries, 4 per topic. Embeddings: sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 on CPU, L2 normalized (recorded in `data/meta.json`). Relevance: all passages of the query's topic. Metric definitions are in DECISIONS.md. Per-query rows are in `results/results.csv`.

| retriever | Recall@1 | Recall@5 | Hit@5 | vectors compared per query |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| flat | 0.613 | 0.518 | 0.812 | 3000 |
| nav | 0.650 | 0.523 | 0.750 | 74 |

Index build cost: 66.4 s for the full `--build` pass (corpus download excluded from repeat runs by the sklearn cache, embedding of 3000 passages plus two k-means levels included). Navigation compared 74 vectors per query on average (20 top-level centroids, about 3 subcluster centroids, about 50 passage vectors in the chosen subtree) against 3000 for flat, a 40x reduction, while scoring slightly higher on Recall@1 (52/80 vs 49/80 queries with an on-topic top hit) and Recall@5 (0.523 vs 0.518). The two retrievers disagree often at rank 1 (only 41 of 80 queries have both retrievers on-topic at rank 1): descending into a subtree acts as a topic prior, which sometimes promotes a mid-ranking on-topic passage to the top and sometimes discards the true best passage. Where navigation lost is Hit@5: flat found at least one on-topic passage in the top 5 for 65/80 queries, nav for 60/80, with 57 queries solved by both, 3 by nav only and 8 by flat only. Every one of those 8 is early pruning. Concrete case: the query "Age of Reason Was: Who has read Rushdie's" (topic alt.atheism) was answered by flat with two alt.atheism passages in the top 5, but nav chose subcluster 35 under top cluster 11 at level 1, a mixed subtree of sci.med, rec.sport.baseball, talk.politics.misc and talk.religion.misc passages, and since nav searches only that subtree no alt.atheism passage was reachable at any rank. A related case is "Best Radar Detector - VALENTINE-1?" (rec.autos), where nav descended into a misc.forsale and sci.electronics subtree; semantically adjacent clusters, but the correct topic was not in them.

Limitations, and the difference from the paper. This is a zero-training approximation: nav scores branches with mean member similarity of k-means clusters, while DecisionRAG (arXiv 2610.35868) trains a hierarchical decision model over 2.68M paragraphs and reports gains over a dense retriever plus an 8B reranker. Here the tree is only three levels deep over 3000 passages, the corpus has clean topic structure that favors clustering, queries are short keyword-like subjects rather than natural questions, and relevance is topic-level, so none of these numbers are comparable to the paper's. Greedy descent is irreversible: no backtracking, no beam, no sibling expansion, so a single wrong centroid comparison at level 1 removes about 1/20th of the corpus from consideration, which is exactly what the 8 flat-only failures show. Whether the pruning rate would grow with tree depth or corpus size, and whether a trained decision model would avoid these branch errors the way the paper's does, are open questions this approximation does not answer. It answers a narrower one: on a structured small corpus, plain hierarchical navigation already matches flat dense retrieval on top-rank topic accuracy at 1/40 of the comparison cost, while losing about 6 points of Hit@5 to irreversible pruning.
